Running an audit method through AI

The dataset is synthetic — a 160-row GRC risk register generated for this exercise, with no employer or production data involved.

AI will produce a defensible-looking analysis from almost any file. None of that makes a result safe in front of an audit committee. This project tested whether the discipline I use in regulatory audit and exams — understand the population, fix the question in writing, run the work, have it challenged independently, build the artifact from what survives — holds when the analysis itself is executed by AI.

The method

Five steps, and why each one exists

Each step closes a specific failure mode: analysing a population I do not understand, choosing the question after seeing the data, leaving my own work unchallenged, and drawing a picture that says more than the evidence does. No step starts until the one before it is written down.

Step 1
Explore
Understand the population before touching it. Which columns are observed, and which are derived from others.
Step 2
Frame
Fix the question in writing, including which population every figure is computed over.
Step 3
Analyze
Run the work, and keep the record of what changed while running it.
Step 4
Verify
Hand it to an adversary that does not know why I did what I did.
Step 5
Visualize
Build only what survived, and design against the wrong conclusion.
How the review was kept independent

The challenge ran on a separate AI agent, given no access to my reasoning from the first three steps and briefed only to attack the conclusions. It worked in two passes: first against the artifact alone, testing whether each claim was actually supported by what was on the page, then against the source file, recomputing everything. A second line of defence is only worth running if the challenger is genuinely independent of the person who did the work.

Transparency

How the AI was used

Split out deliberately, because it is the question anyone should ask of work like this.

Claude (Opus 5) did

  • Wrote and ran every profiling and analysis script in pandas, plus the statistical testing — chi-squared, Fisher exact, Kruskal–Wallis, Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel, Clopper–Pearson intervals, Bonferroni correction.
  • Generated the dashboard and all five records natively in code, so no rendering step can alter a number.
  • Ran the adversarial challenge as a separate agent, against a brief and a blindness rule I set.
  • Drafted every document.

I set, directed and own

  • The question, audience, scope boundary and denominator rule — fixed in writing before analysis, so findings answer a question defined in advance.
  • The two-pass review structure and what each pass was permitted to see.
  • What could be charted, what had to be withdrawn, what could headline, and the confidence stated on each record.
  • Every finding, withdrawal and recorded error, signed on the face of each document.

Evidence

What the review withdrew

Every number in the analysis recomputed exactly. The arithmetic was correct throughout. What the challenge stopped were claims resting on the wrong group of records, a comparison that was really random variation, and a measure distorted by its own formula.

What was stoppedEffect
A headline rate calculated over records that could never have been late — 34 items had closed before their deadline arrived 85% → 78.3%
A main finding written up as settled while a note on the same page recorded the test as outstanding Stated without evidence
A “four times higher” difference that was the best and worst of six categories — seven items against two Withdrawn, p = 0.30
A severity measure divided by the time allowed to fix the issue, which is set tighter for severe items by policy Ranking reversed
A blind spot in the source file: the overdue counter resets on closure, hiding 18 late closures 28.4% → 44.0%
A finding the review itself raised turned out to be wrong. It stayed in the record with the correction. Kept in the log

Scope

What this does not claim

Read this before quoting any figure

The dataset is synthetic and three of its fields are not independent. Testing showed control effectiveness is coupled to status and to nothing else in the file — not inherent score, not residual reduction, not exposure.

The pack demonstrates a method. It is not evidence about how controls behave in a real institution. That sits in the dashboard masthead rather than the footer, because it changes how every figure below should be read.
